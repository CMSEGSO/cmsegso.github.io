02 · Your degree map

Academics

The CMSE PhD combines core training in computational and data science, additional coursework related to your research interests, and doctoral dissertation research. This page gives a high-level overview of the standard requirements.

For exceptions, dual degrees, transferred graduate coursework, qualifier details, or unusual programs of study, consult the official CMSE Graduate Handbook and the CMSE Graduate Director.

  • Passing the comprehensive examination

Keep these separate: the 30 coursework credits and the CMSE 999 research credits are separate requirements. CMSE 999 does not count toward the 30 credits of non-research coursework.

02 / Complete the coursework picture

What about the other 18 credits?

30 total coursework credits−12 core credits=18+ additional credits

For a standard student without applicable prior graduate coursework, the four core courses provide 12 credits, so students normally need at least 18 additional coursework credits. If those courses were all 3-credit courses, that would usually mean about six additional courses.

The requirement is based on credits, not a fixed number of courses. Choose additional coursework in consultation with your dissertation or guidance committee. Courses at the 800 level and above typically fulfill this requirement.

Some lower-level coursework may be allowed, but no coursework below the 400 level may count, no more than 6 credits below the 800 level may count, and below-800 coursework requires appropriate approval. Check the Graduate Handbook for unusual cases.

03 / Shape your expertise

What is a cognate?

A cognate is a coherent group of courses outside or beyond the four CMSE core subjects that gives you deeper expertise relevant to your research.

The program should normally include at least 12 credits addressing a single general topic or subject area. A cognate might be an application domain, mathematics, statistics, computer science, or another computational or data-science area relevant to your work.

Choose the cognate with your dissertation committee, or with the Graduate Director before your committee has formed. Your committee ultimately helps determine which coursework makes sense for your program of study.

04 / Flexible graduate coursework

Special topics & independent study

CMSE 890

Selected Topics

CMSE 890 is a variable-topic graduate course. Topics vary by semester and instructor, with 1–4 credits per enrollment and up to 12 credits across CMSE 890 enrollments.

These are real graduate courses on specialized subjects and can help build a program around your research interests. A particular offering is not automatically guaranteed to satisfy every student’s cognate requirement; your program of study still needs appropriate approval.

CMSE 891

Independent Study

CMSE 891 is an independent study completed with departmental approval. It carries 1–4 credits per enrollment, with a maximum of 6 credits across enrollments.

Considering an independent study? Confirm with your advisor, guidance committee, or the CMSE Graduate Director that the credits will apply to your planned degree requirements.

05 / Research toward the dissertation

What is CMSE 999?

Doctoral Dissertation Research

CMSE 999 is not a normal lecture course. It represents academic credit for the research being conducted toward your doctoral dissertation.

24–36CMSE 999 credits required
separate from coursework

CMSE PhD students must complete at least 24 and no more than 36 total credits of CMSE 999. These credits are not a substitute for the additional courses needed to reach 30 coursework credits.

Students can enroll in a variable number of CMSE 999 credits depending on their stage in the program and enrollment needs. MSU currently considers doctoral students academically full-time at 6 credits before comprehensive exams and 1 credit after comprehensive exams while actively engaged in dissertation research.

Assistantship, fellowship, visa, insurance, and other enrollment rules can add requirements. Not sure how many credits to register for this semester? Check with the CMSE Graduate Team or your advisor.

06 / The road to graduation

Major PhD milestones

Core
training→Guidance
committee→Comprehensive
exam→Dissertation
research→Defense

Core / qualifying requirement

Demonstrate competency in the four CMSE core subject areas.

Dissertation committee

Students are expected to form a committee by the end of the first year. The committee helps choose coursework, guide research, administer the comprehensive exam, and evaluate progress.

Comprehensive examination

The exam evaluates research-area knowledge, written and oral scientific communication, and a realistic dissertation plan. It should be completed no later than the end of the third year and at least six months before the defense.

Annual progress

Meet with your dissertation committee at least annually and complete required annual progress reporting.

Responsible Conduct of Research

All CMSE PhD students must complete Responsible Conduct of Research training.

Dissertation and defense

Complete and defend an original dissertation in computational and/or data science before your dissertation committee, including the required 24–36 CMSE 999 credits.

07 / When the standard map does not fit

Your requirements may be different if...

  • You entered CMSE with previous graduate coursework or already have a master’s degree.
  • You are pursuing a dual PhD or trying to waive a core requirement.
  • You want non-standard coursework to count or are transferring graduate credits.
  • You are unsure whether an independent study applies to your plan.

These situations have specific rules. Rather than relying on this overview, consult the CMSE Graduate Handbook and talk with the CMSE Graduate Director or your guidance committee.
