Proposal

Funding allocation and rationale

$1,500

Wellness Center

The Wellness Center provides free essential supplies to graduate students, creating an incentive for students to visit the office while reducing the financial burden associated with everyday necessities. Building on previous years, which focused primarily on household supplies, this year's initiative will expand to include school supplies to better support students' academic needs.

Success will be evaluated through student engagement indicators such as participation rates, repeat visits, and resource use. Impact will be assessed through department reach, student feedback, and evidence that supplies helped alleviate financial or academic challenges. The program also shows incoming and prospective students that graduate student well-being is actively supported.

Whillans et al. (2017) found that spending money to reduce everyday burdens and save time is associated with greater well-being and life satisfaction. Tsang et al. (2023) found that reducing family-work conflict is associated with higher work engagement, self-efficacy, and productivity.

$2,000

Community Building

These efforts are intended to affect the department at large, not only students who are already involved in GSO activities. Free lunches, catered gatherings, and resources that make working in the department more comfortable and productive create low-barrier opportunities for students to come together, learn about available resources, share feedback, and feel more connected to the department.

Success and impact will be measured through attendance, repeat participation, department-wide representation, and student feedback on whether these efforts improved their day-to-day experience.

$1,500

Professional Development

The GSO will invite speakers from academia, industry, and national labs; host workshops and discussions; and help students use available resources effectively. This includes increasing awareness of department resources such as the Wellness Center, standing desks, printers, shared workspaces, and HPC resources, as well as student-accessible resources including GitHub Education, Overleaf Premium, Gemini, and other professional or academic tools.

Practical workshops will cover topics such as creating a free professional website, using the command line interface, and accessing HPC resources. Success and impact will be measured through attendance, engagement, increased resource awareness, and evidence that students are making greater use of available resources.
