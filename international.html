International Students

Practical resources for international CMSE graduate students adjusting to life at MSU and in East Lansing.

For official international-student guidance, visit the MSU Office for International Students and Scholars (OISS).

01

Taxes & Social Security

International students may have different U.S. tax filing requirements. OISS maintains information about taxes and tax-preparation resources for international students.

Use International student tax resources and MSU VITA for current guidance. If you are working in the United States, you may also need a Social Security Number. See Social Security Number information.

02

Banking & Finances

Most students will want to open a U.S. bank account after arriving. A checking account is used for everyday deposits and payments, while a debit card spends money directly from that account.

A credit card lets you borrow money and repay it later. Paying the balance on time can help build a U.S. credit history. Keep track of your balance and avoid overdrawing your checking account.

06

Living in East Lansing

Food

Use campus dining and local grocery stores, and learn about the MSU Student Food Bank.

Michigan weather

Michigan winters can bring freezing temperatures, snow, ice, and fast-changing weather. Plan for a warm coat, layers, a hat and gloves, and shoes or boots with traction. Do not completely turn off the heat in your apartment during freezing weather, including when traveling; frozen pipes can cause serious damage.

Housing

Find housing around MSU and East Lansing through International student housing information.

Safety & scams

Be cautious about unexpected demands for money or personal information. Review U.S. laws and safety and Avoiding scams.

Everyday U.S. Life

Tipping is common at full-service restaurants, bars, hair salons, and similar service businesses. Around 20% is a common restaurant tip, although expectations vary by situation.
