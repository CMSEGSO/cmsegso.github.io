First Year Survival Guide

01 / Find your footing

Space & supplies

The CMSE graduate space is collaborative and flexible. Learn the local norms, then make the space work for you.

Front office staff

Stop by the front office when you need office supplies, and take some time to get to know the staff.

The original guide includes a staff directory link; it can be added here when the current URL is confirmed.

Rooms and quiet corners

  • Reserve a conference room for study or research by checking the schedule first, then emailing the front office.
  • As an employee and graduate assistant, you can reserve rooms in the Engineering building. Check this link out
  • Small private pods in the first-floor elevator lobby are useful for virtual meetings.
  • The College of Engineering graduate lounge in EB 2420 has free snacks.

Shared-space norms

  • Fridges are open use. Label anything staying for multiple days with your name and the date.
  • Whiteboards, pillars, and study rooms are available for collaborative work.
  • Erase boards when you are done. Mark any work that should stay with “DNE” (Do Not Erase), and photograph important work in case it is removed by mistake.

02 / Prepare for the season

Surviving winter

Lansing winters are manageable with the right layers, shoes, and a little planning.

  • When buying a winter coat, consider buying a size up or trying it on over a hoodie.
  • Nice winter boots help on cold mornings. Many CMSE graduate students keep desk shoes such as slippers or sandals so they do not overheat in the office.
  • Lansing is one of the cloudiest metro areas in the country. A sunshine lamp in your apartment or office can support your circadian rhythm, and Vitamin D supplements may help with seasonal depression.

04 / Find your people

Involvement & communication

  • Join the CMSE graduate Slack workspace.
  • Come to lunch! Most days students eat lunch at the first floor kitchenette.
  • Meet the GSO Executive Board and connect with GSO leadership.

First Year Liaison

The current FYL listed in the guide is Matthew Blake. The FYL is the main point of contact for first-year students.

The position is elected at the beginning of each spring semester with two goals:

  • Be the voice for first-year students and connect them with the larger graduate student community.
  • Learn the department’s administrative mechanics.

Reach out to Matthew or another GSO Executive Board member if this role interests you.

05 / Build a good working relationship

Advisor mentorship

The Graduate School has developed Guidelines for Graduate Student Mentoring and Advising Talk with your advisor about this at least once during graduate school; earlier is better.

Meaningful conversations about expectations, research goals, and career ambitions are important to a successful student-advisor relationship. Talk with your advisor about your Individual Development Plan (IDP) and schedule a meeting to discuss it in detail. An IDP is a living document, so revisit it annually to make sure you still agree on the goals.

If you are unsure how to proceed or are having trouble, MSU’s Office of the University Ombudsperson is a resource for advice, guidelines, and understanding the paths available to you.

06 / Learn together

Classes

  • First-year core courses are difficult. GSO cannot remove the workload, but it can help decrease the stress.
  • GSO offers study breaks around midterms and finals. Taking time to decompress matters.
  • Your classmates are likely struggling with some of the same things. Make a study group, work through homework together, and ask for help.
  • Professors may feel intimidating, but they want to see you succeed. Stop by office hours with questions.
  • Older students have created preliminary material for some core courses, including CMSE 821 and CMSE 823. Use it to refresh skills or develop new ones.

Graduate students are eligible for a semester parking pass. It provides access to employee lots south of the Red Cedar River. Ask someone on the GSO leadership team for help with the details.
